# Little Lanterns — Shia Islamic Curriculum Integration Standard

## Core principle

Islam is not a separate subject corner in Little Lanterns. The Qur'an and the teachings of the Prophet Muhammad and his Ahl al-Bayt (peace be upon them) should shape the way children approach **every** subject: language, mathematics, science, nature, history, communication, creativity and everyday problem-solving.

This does not mean adding a religious sentence artificially to every paragraph. Each lesson must make a meaningful, age-appropriate connection between the skill being learned and an authentic Islamic teaching, value, question or way of looking at the world.

## Required lesson structure

Every published chapter must contain:

1. **Learn:** teach the subject accurately and at the child's level.
2. **Notice:** invite observation, curiosity, reasoning or practice.
3. **Islamic lens:** make a natural connection to a Qur'anic verse or a traceable Shia hadith/teaching from the Ahl al-Bayt.
4. **Reflect and act:** ask how the knowledge can help the child become more truthful, just, grateful, responsible, thoughtful or useful to others.
5. **Sources:** provide an exact Qur'an reference or a named Shia hadith source and location. Clearly distinguish scripture, hadith, explanation and original fiction.

The reader should show a concise reflection in the lesson itself, not hide the Islamic connection only in a bibliography.

## Subject-specific guidance

### Mathematics

Teach mathematics as a real discipline: number sense, arithmetic, patterns, geometry, measurement, time, money, fractions, estimation and logical reasoning.

Possible Islamic connections include:

- **Fair measure and balance:** Qur'an 55:7–9 teaches balance and not transgressing in the balance. Qur'an 83:1–3 warns against giving people less than their due in measure. These are meaningful connections for measurement, comparison, units and honest calculations.
- **Honesty with quantities:** use examples involving fair sharing, accurate counting, keeping promises and not changing an answer to gain an unfair advantage.
- **Order and patterns:** invite children to notice patterns and order in the world, while keeping mathematical proofs and explanations grounded in mathematics.

Do not claim that a Qur'anic verse is a substitute for a mathematical proof or that a modern formula is encoded in a verse.

### Science and nature

Teach science through accurate observation, evidence, experiments, models and age-appropriate explanations. Children should learn how to ask questions, test ideas safely and distinguish what is known from what is still uncertain.

Possible Islamic connections include:

- **Reflection on creation:** Qur'an 3:190–191 encourages reflection on the creation of the heavens and the earth.
- **Creation and human responsibility:** Qur'an 45:13 invites reflection on what Allah has made available in the heavens and earth.
- **Water and living things:** Qur'an 21:30, 16:10–14 and 24:45 can be used carefully when lessons explore water, life and living creatures.
- **Care and gratitude:** connect environmental stewardship, avoiding waste and helping living things with Qur'an 7:31, 6:141 and 28:77 where relevant.

Never present a scientific hypothesis as Qur'anic fact, force a modern scientific interpretation onto a verse, or label a lesson a scientific miracle without sound scholarly grounds. Explain the science on its own evidence, then offer the Qur'anic reflection as a way to deepen wonder, gratitude and responsibility.

### Language, reading and communication

Use stories and vocabulary to practise truthfulness, careful speech, listening, humility, kindness and conveying knowledge responsibly. Relevant foundations include Qur'an 49:11–12, 17:53 and 33:70, alongside verified Shia narrations about knowledge and good character.

### History, society and problem-solving

Use historically careful accounts and distinguish established events from reports whose details or grading are disputed. Teach justice, trustworthiness, courage, patience, consultation and service through Qur'anic guidance and reliable teachings of the Ahl al-Bayt.

## Source and attribution rules

- Qur'anic references must include surah and ayah numbers. Link ayah references to the QuranMemorizer deep link format:
  `https://mahashemi.github.io/QuranMemorizer/#/surah/{surah}/ayah/{ayah}`.
- Hadith references must identify the Shia collection and a usable location (book/chapter/hadith, volume/page, letter or sermon as appropriate). Link to a primary text or a reputable Shia reference when available.
- A collection citation alone does not prove that every report in it is authentic. Do not silently call a report sahih. Note disputed grading or avoid using the report as definitive proof.
- Do not invent Arabic wording, translations, chains, hadith numbers, historical details or quotations. If the exact source cannot be verified, label the point as an educational reflection rather than attributing it to an Imam.
- Original child characters and classroom situations are fictional illustrations. They are not evidence for a religious teaching.
- Keep the child's explanation simple, but keep the source record precise enough for a parent or teacher to check.

## Quality checklist before a chapter is published

- [ ] Subject content is accurate and age-appropriate.
- [ ] The Islamic connection is natural, meaningful and visible in the lesson.
- [ ] At least one Qur'anic or traceable Shia hadith source is recorded.
- [ ] Scripture, hadith, scholarly explanation and fictional illustration are clearly distinguished.
- [ ] No unsupported religious or scientific claim is presented as fact.
- [ ] A child can explain one useful idea or action after completing the lesson.

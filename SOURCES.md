# Little Lanterns — Qur'an and Shia Hadith Source Register

## Editorial rule

Every religious teaching, moral conclusion, or statement about belief in a reading chapter must be traceable to the Qur'an or to a named Shia hadith source. A source note must identify the surah and verses, or the hadith collection and location. Child-friendly paraphrases must never be presented as verbatim translations or quotations.

A fictional child and an everyday situation may be used only as an illustration of a teaching already established by the Qur'an or a Shia hadith. The fictional scene is not evidence for the teaching. Do not invent dialogue for a Prophet or an Imam.

When a report's chain or grading is disputed, say so or avoid relying on it as a definitive proof. A citation to a hadith collection does not, by itself, mean every report in that collection is authentic.

## Chapter-by-chapter source register

### 1. A Special Dream — Prophet Yusuf
- Qur'an 12:4–6: Yusuf's dream, Ya'qub's advice, and Allah's promise to teach him.
- Qur'an 12:18 and 12:90: patience and the later affirmation of patience and doing good.
- The story is an educational retelling, not a verbatim translation.
- Text: [Qur'an, Surah Yusuf](https://quran.com/12).

### 2. Anger: Control Is Strength
- Qur'an 3:134: those who restrain anger and pardon people.
- Al-Kafi, vol. 2, p. 110, hadith 7: report from Imam al-Baqir on restraining anger while able to act on it.
- Al-Kafi, vol. 2, p. 303, hadith 3: Imam al-Sadiq's statement that anger is a key to evil.
- Tuhaf al-'Uqul, no. 286: Imam al-Baqir's teaching that there is no strength like restraining anger.
- Reference guide with Arabic and citations: [Mizan al-Hikmah — Anger](https://al-islam.org/mizan-al-hikmah-scale-wisdom/anger).
- The Sami-and-sister situation is a fictional illustration. The lesson is restraint and pardon; no breathing technique or unsupported psychological claim is taught as a religious instruction.

### 3. Use This World for Good
- Qur'an 7:31: eat and drink, but do not be wasteful.
- Qur'an 28:77: seek the Hereafter through what Allah has given while not forgetting one's share of this world; do good and do not seek corruption.
- Qur'an 6:141: eat of the provision and do not waste.
- Al-Kafi, vol. 5, Book 2, Chapter 3, especially hadiths 8–10: Imam al-Sadiq teaches that worldly means can be used in the service of the Hereafter.
- Primary-text page: [Al-Kafi — Using the Worldly Resources as Means for Success in the Hereafter](https://thaqalayn.net/chapter/5/2/3).

### 4. Jihad al-Nafs: The Inner Struggle
- Qur'an 91:7–10: the soul, its moral awareness, and success through purification.
- Qur'an 79:40–41: restraining the self from wrongful desire.
- The report about the greater jihad and the struggle against the self is transmitted through Imam al-Sadiq from the Prophet and cited as an al-Kafi report in the source below. It is not a licence for violence; this chapter uses the term only for moral self-struggle.
- Reference and Arabic text: [Imam Khomeini, Forty Hadith — First Hadith: Jihad of the Self](https://al-islam.org/forty-hadith-exposition-second-revised-edition-sayyid-ruhullah-musawi-khomeini/first-hadith-jihad).

### 5. Namaz: Remembering Allah
- Qur'an 20:14: establish prayer for Allah's remembrance.
- Qur'an 29:45: prayer restrains indecency and wrongdoing.
- Nahj al-Balagha, Letter 47: Imam Ali's counsel to remain mindful of prayer and his description of it as the pillar of religion.
- Explanatory reference: [Ayatullah Sistani — Salat: The Ritual Prayer](https://al-islam.org/code-practice-muslims-west-sayyid-ali-hussaini-sistani/salat-ritual-prayer).

### 6. The Soul: What We Know
- Qur'an 17:85: human knowledge of the spirit is limited.
- Qur'an 91:7–10: the soul and its purification.
- Al-Kafi, vol. 3, Book 3, Chapter 91: reports concerning the souls of believers. The online edition records differing hadith gradings; the chapter does not rely on disputed details to make claims about the soul.
- Primary-text page: [Al-Kafi — Chapters about the Souls of Believing People](https://thaqalayn.net/chapter/3/3/91).

### 7. Learning with Humility
- Al-Kafi, Book of the Merit of Knowledge (Kitab Fadl al-'Ilm), report 4/38: Imam al-Sadiq says seeking knowledge is a duty.
- Al-Kafi, Book of the Merit of Knowledge, report 8/65: Imam al-Sadiq teaches that knowledge should be accompanied by forbearance, dignity and humility.
- Reference with Arabic and citations: [Selections from Usul al-Kafi — The Book of the Merit of Knowledge](https://al-islam.org/al-tawhid/general-al-tawhid/selections-usul-al-kafi-al-kulayni/2-book-merit-knowledge-kitab-fadl-al-ilm).
- The Zaynab classroom scene is a fictional illustration, not a historical report.
- Al-Kafi, vol. 1, p. 48, hadith 4 (as cited in Mizan al-Hikmah, Knowledge): the stages of knowledge include listening, learning, acting upon it and sharing it.

### 8. The First 313 Companions
- Kitab al-Ghayba by al-Nu'mani, Book 2, Chapter 20, reports 5–8: reports about 313 companions gathering in Mecca.
- One report connects the gathering with Qur'an 2:148.
- Primary-text page: [Kitab al-Ghayba — The Army of Anger, reports 5–8](https://thaqalayn.net/chapter/22/2/20).
- Important limits: these reports do not establish the Hilla forty-guests anecdote, do not quote the Imam as saying that 313 people make reappearance compulsory, and do not establish the exact phrase that he has been waiting 1,200 years. Those claims are therefore not taught as Qur'an or hadith in this chapter. The narrations about 313 should be read in their wider context.

### 9. Qur'an: Surah Al-Asr
- Qur'an 103:1–3. The chapter's four central teachings are taken directly from the surah: faith, good deeds, mutual encouragement to truth, and mutual encouragement to patience.
- Text: [Qur'an, Surah Al-Asr](https://quran.com/103).

### 10. Qur'an: Surah Al-Kawthar
- Qur'an 108:1–3. The explanation stays with the surah's wording about al-Kawthar, prayer and sacrifice for Allah.
- Do not state one interpretation of al-Kawthar as the only meaning without citing a recognised Shia tafsir.
- Text: [Qur'an, Surah Al-Kawthar](https://quran.com/108).

### 11. Qur'an: Al-Falaq and An-Nas
- Qur'an 113:1–5 and 114:1–6. The lessons are restricted to the harms named in the two surahs and seeking refuge in Allah.
- Text: [Qur'an, Surah Al-Falaq](https://quran.com/113) and [Surah An-Nas](https://quran.com/114).

## Editorial corrections made in this audit

- Replaced the unsourced anger advice with Qur'an 3:134 and named Shia hadith references.
- Removed invented claims about what a child "must" do when angry; fictional scenes now illustrate restraint and pardon only.
- Reframed "The World Is a Trust" as "Use This World for Good" to avoid presenting an unsourced slogan as a direct scriptural statement.
- Grounded jihad al-nafs in Qur'an 91:7–10 and 79:40–41, with the cited Shia transmission of the greater-jihad report.
- Limited claims about the soul to what the Qur'an and cited hadith support.
- Replaced the Allama Hilli biography chapter with direct teachings on seeking knowledge from al-Kafi, so the chapter's teaching is not derived from an unsourced biography.
- Replaced the Hilla forty-guests devotional anecdote with reports about the 313 companions from Kitab al-Ghayba. The Hilla anecdote and the exact 1,200-year wording are not established by the cited Qur'an or hadith reports, so they are not used as religious evidence.
- Kept the Qur'anic chapters tied to the actual verses being explained.
